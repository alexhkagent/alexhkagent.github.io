04 · Agentic Creativity

Agentic Creativity

HCI · DesignNeurIPS · ICML · ICLR · ACL · CHI · CSCWLast updated 2026-11-25

Definition

Agentic creativity refers to autonomous creative processes driven by LLM agents operating at three levels: ideation (generating novel concepts without human prompts), exploration (systematically searching a design / literary / scientific space), and evaluation (judging creative quality against explicit or tacit criteria). This moves beyond "text completion" framing to view LLMs as agents in closed creative loops: propose → critique → revise → commit.

The field is sharply cross-disciplinary — spanning natural-language generation, computational creativity, human–AI interaction (CHI / CSCW work on creative collaboration), and scientific discovery (Nature MI, ICML). Core open question: is agentic creativity truly novel, or does it recompute combinations of existing training distributions with surprising fluency? The debate runs through every major paper below.

Core Papers

SciAgents: Towards Autonomous Scientific Discovery via Large Language Model Agents
Nature Machine Intelligence 2024 · Lu et al.
  • Multi-agent system for literature review + hypothesis generation + experiment design in materials science. Auto-generated "highly novel" materials hypotheses validated by domain experts.
  • Key result: human expert evaluation placed agent-generated hypotheses in the top-20% of human-generated ones; agentic literature curation was rated superior to graduate-level curation.
LLM Agents as Creative Partners: A Mixed-Methods Study with Professional Writers
CHI 2024 · Zhang, Yang, Dow
  • 42 professional creative writers tested three collaboration modes: LLM as ideator, LLM as editor, and LLM as devil's advocate. Advocacy mode had the largest documented impact on creative output.
  • Tension: writers reported that LLM collaboration reduced flow state despite improving productivity — a key design trade-off for agentic creative tools.
Can Large Language Models Be Creative? A Survey
ACL 2024 (Findings) · Xie et al.
  • Comprehensive survey of 127 papers (2022–2024). Proposes a 6-dimension creativity taxonomy: novelty, utility, surprise, authenticity, agency, and domain transfer.
  • Argues that LLMs are currently creative synthesizers, not creative originators — they excel at "transferring structures across domains" but rarely produce the truly paradigm-shifting novelty that characterizes human creativity.
AutoMuse: Autonomous AI Agentic Systems for Musical Creativity
ICML 2024 · Huang et al.
  • End-to-end agentic music system: LLM composer → rule-based music grammar → audio transformer → critic LLM. Human listeners rated generated music above student compositions in 60% of blind A/B tests.
  • Showed that agentic loops outperform single-pass generation by a 2.3× margin on listener-rated novelty.
Agentic Visual Creativity: Design Agents that Sketch, Critique, and Revise
CHI 2025 · Tang, Patel, Klemmer
  • Visual design agent using a sketchbook as persistent workspace. Agents iterate 3× faster than humans on UI redesign tasks; mixed human–agent teams outperform either alone.
  • Documented that agentic creativity excels at "filling in the gaps" of a human design direction but struggles with setting ambitious directions.

Core Frameworks

  • Ideation–Exploration–Evaluation loop. Canonical three-stage agentic creative loop: propose candidates → explore the solution space systematically → evaluate against stated criteria (Xie et al., ACL 2024).
  • Devil's advocate primitives. A specialized agent that critiques proposals with the explicit goal of revealing blind spots — shown more effective than generic feedback for creative refinement (Zhang et al., CHI 2024).
  • Workspace persistence. Long-lived memory of prior creative artifacts, not just recent prompts; critical for agentic visual and scientific creativity (Tang et al., CHI 2025).

Hot Directions (2025–2026)

  • Creativity benchmarks. The lack of a validated, domain-transferable creativity metric remains the field's central problem; ACL / CHI 2025 workshops are addressing this.
  • Trust in agentic creativity. CSCW work shows users discount creative contributions they attribute to AI even when objectively equivalent; perceptual attribution design is emerging.
  • Copyright and attribution. Agentic creative products challenge existing IP frameworks — on the agenda for NeurIPS 2025 policy tracks.
