01 · Caricature Amplification

Caricature Amplification and Harnessing

AI SafetyNeurIPS · ICML · ICLR · ACL · EMNLP · AAAI · CHI · FT50/UTD24 · Nature · Science · PNASLast updated 2026-11-25

Definition

Caricature amplification refers to the tendency of large language models, when prompted to simulate or describe a specific demographic, identity, or viewpoint, to systematically flatten that group into an exaggerated, two-dimensional "caricature." The phenomenon is documented in persona-steered generation, demographic simulation for social science surveys, role-play safety testing, and agentic self-expression. Two diagnostic criteria — individuation (within-group diversity is erased) and exaggeration (between-group stereotypes are amplified beyond empirical reality) — jointly define caricature in the CoMPosT diagnostic framework (Cheng et al., EMNLP 2023).

Caricature is not a subtype of generic toxicity or hate speech: it produces text that reads fluent and socially plausible yet is systematically misleading at the group level. It is amplified by instruction tuning that rewards fluent persona-consistent output without penalizing flattening, and by RLHF that anchors "helpful" responses to a narrow representation of the target group.

In management and economics, the same failure surfaces as distorted synthetic respondents and as symbolic compliance in LLM-assisted evaluation — visible stereotypes are suppressed while the underlying evaluative logic is left intact.

Core Papers

CoMPosT: Characterizing and Evaluating Caricature in LLM Simulations
EMNLP 2023 · Cheng, Piccardi, Yang
  • Introduces the CoMPosT framework (Context · Model · Persona · Topic) with two measurable caricature criteria: individuation and exaggeration.
  • GPT-4 simulations of political and marginalized groups show significantly higher caricature rates than neutral groups; demographic "minority" status alone predicts caricature severity.
  • Caricature is distinct from toxicity — measured text is rated fluent, yet empirically wrong about group-level properties.
Bias and Volatility: A Statistical Framework for Evaluating LLM Stereotypes and Generation Inconsistency
NeurIPS 2024 · Datasets & Benchmarks Track
  • Distinguishes systematic bias (amplification of group stereotypes) from generation volatility (same prompt produces wildly different stereotype strength).
  • Showed that temperature settings — routinely used to control creativity — non-linearly amplify caricature: both near-zero and mid-range temperatures produce stronger group flattening.
Evaluating Large Language Model Biases in Persona-Steered Generation
Findings of ACL 2024 · Liu, Diab, Fried
  • Large-scale evaluation of 13 LLMs across 40 persona dimensions shows that alignment fine-tuning increases caricature relative to base models for many persona classes.
  • Instruct models produce 2.4× higher exaggeration rates on gendered personas than their base counterparts, despite lower toxicity.
Bias Amplification: Language Models as Increasingly Biased Media
ACL SRW / IJCNLP-AACL 2024 · Wang, Wu, Zhang, Jain, Guan, Koshiyama
  • Characterizes the "amplification loop": LLM-generated persona text re-enters training data and systematically amplifies prior biases over successive model generations.
  • Proposes a closed-loop audit procedure (prompt → collect → retrain → re-audit) to measure amplification velocity.

Management & Economics Perspectives

Recent FT50 and UTD24 work treats caricature less as toxic text than as a validity problem in synthetic respondents and a fairness problem in LLM-assisted evaluation. The literature is young, but the first multi-venue cluster appeared in 2024–2026.

Bias In, Symbolic Compliance Out? GPT's Reliance on Gender and Race in Strategic Evaluations
Strategic Management Journal · 2026 · Botelho & Wang
  • Across 26,000 GPT evaluations of startup pitches that differed only in founder name, underrepresented minorities were not systematically scored lower — but they were less likely to be ranked last without becoming more likely to win.
  • The pattern is "symbolic compliance": GPT corrected overt identity-based bias more readily than bias framed as neutral business critique, and corrections were small in magnitude.
  • Caricature can therefore survive as a ceiling on final selection rather than as an explicitly negative score — a form of amplification that aggregate score comparisons miss.
Simulating Strategic Interactions with AI Agents
Strategic Management Journal · 2026 · Tranchero, Brenninkmeijer, Murugan & Nagaraj
  • Introduces a framework for running strategy experiments with LLM agents as synthetic subjects, applied to the exploration–exploitation dilemma.
  • Simulations reproduce core human patterns and identify where those patterns weaken or reverse, positioning agents as "model organisms" for hypothesis generation.
  • Treats external validity as the central limitation: a persona is a modeling choice, not a population sample.
Bank Run, Interrupted: Modeling Deposit Withdrawals with Generative AI
Management Science · 2026 · Kazinnik
  • Builds a synthetic depositor population with assigned demographic attributes, exposes it to a panic post, and validates withdrawal intent against human benchmarks before testing message variants.
  • Shows the constructive case: persona simulation can avoid caricature inside a narrow, validated design space and then support counterfactual policy tests.
  • The boundary condition is explicit — validation against human data is what separates calibrated simulation from stereotype projection.
Frontiers: Can Large Language Models Capture Human Preferences?
Marketing Science · 2024 · Goli & Singh
  • GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 are less patient than humans in intertemporal choice; GPT-3.5 follows a lexicographic rule for earlier rewards that human decision makers do not.
  • Measured discount rates shift with prompt language, with German and Mandarin producing greater patience — evidence that "preferences" are systematic artifacts rather than stable human traits.
  • Extends caricature beyond demographic description: models also flatten decision rules into simplified, non-human heuristics.
Database Report: Twin-2K-500 — Building Digital Twins of over 2,000 People
Marketing Science · 2025 · Toubia, Gui, Peng, Merlau, et al.
  • Public dataset of 2,058 U.S. participants, 500+ questions across demographics, psychology, behavioral economics, and pricing, collected over four waves.
  • Initial analyses show that digital twins can predict behavior well at both individual and aggregate levels, providing a benchmark for grounding personas in real response distributions.
  • Offers the main methodological antidote to flattening: ground each persona in individual-level data rather than in group labels alone.
AI–Human Hybrids for Marketing Research: Leveraging LLMs as Collaborators
Journal of Marketing · 2025 · Arora, Chakraborty & Nishimura
  • LLMs can generate sample characteristics, simulate respondents, and conduct or moderate in-depth interviews; human–LLM hybrids outperform both LLM-only and human-only workflows.
  • Frames synthetic respondents as complements that require human oversight, not substitutes for sampling a real population.

General Science Journals

Nature, Science, and PNAS have become the main venues for population-scale evidence on how models reproduce and amplify stereotypes, and for the methodological debate over LLMs as human surrogates.

Age and Gender Distortion in Online Media and Large Language Models
Nature · 2025 · Guilbeault, Delecourt & Desikan
  • Analysis of nearly 1.4 million images across Google, Wikipedia, IMDb, Flickr, and YouTube finds women represented as younger than men across occupations and social roles, most strongly in high-status roles.
  • Prompted to write 40,000 resumes for 54 occupations, ChatGPT estimated female candidates to be 1.6 years younger on average and rated older male candidates as higher quality.
  • Direct evidence that models do not merely inherit online distortions — they actively reproduce them in evaluative output.
Take Caution in Using LLMs as Human Surrogates
PNAS · 2025 · Gao, Lee, Burtch & Fazelpour
  • Across eight LLMs and 1,000 sessions each in the 11–20 money request game, response distributions diverge significantly from human participants; newer and larger models are not necessarily more human-like.
  • Prompt language, framing ("rational" vs. "like a human"), in-context examples, and fine-tuning all shift outputs; supplying detailed demographics does not restore human-like diversity.
  • Concludes that LLM surrogates require validation against human data and should not be treated as population samples by default.
Explicitly Unbiased Large Language Models Still Form Biased Associations
PNAS · 2025 · Bai, Wang, Sucholutsky & Griffiths
  • Behavior-based measures reveal pervasive stereotype associations in eight value-aligned models across race, gender, religion, and health — 21 stereotypes in total.
  • Biases persist in models that appear unbiased on standard evaluations and produce sizable effects on downstream discriminatory decisions.
  • Shows that alignment suppresses explicit expression more reliably than it removes the underlying association.
Emergent Social Conventions and Collective Bias in LLM Populations
Science Advances · 2025 · Ashery, Aiello & Baronchelli
  • Decentralized populations of LLM agents spontaneously converge on shared naming conventions without central coordination.
  • Strong collective biases can emerge even when no individual agent is biased, and a committed minority of adversarial agents can impose an alternative convention on the population.
  • Reframes amplification as a population-level dynamic: auditing single responses cannot capture what repeated agent interaction produces.
A Foundation Model to Predict and Capture Human Cognition
Nature · 2025 · Binz et al. (Centaur)
  • Centaur is trained on data from 160 psychological experiments and predicts human behavior in new experiments expressed in natural language.
  • Provides the strongest current counterpoint to persona prompting: grounding simulation in individual experimental data improves behavioral fidelity.
  • Not evidence that generic persona prompting is safe — its advantage comes from task-specific human data, and coverage remains bounded by the experiments it has seen.

Hot Directions (2025–2026)

  • Demographic ground-truth benchmarks. Most caricature evaluations compare model output to "common sense" stereotypes rather than empirical social-science data (population surveys, census, sociological studies). Individual-level datasets such as Twin-2K-500 (Marketing Science 2025) now provide a public benchmark for this work.
  • Population-level amplification. Science Advances (2025) shows that collective bias can emerge in LLM populations even when no individual agent is biased, shifting attention from single responses to interaction dynamics and committed minorities.
  • Symbolic compliance in evaluation. Strategic Management Journal (2026) finds that aligned evaluators avoid visible discrimination without improving final selection — a reminder that lower stereotype scores need not mean fairer outcomes.
  • Persona-individuation training objectives. Contrastive and multi-task objectives that reward within-group diversity (e.g., prompting the same persona across 10 counterfactual contexts) while penalizing between-group exaggeration.
  • LLM-as-judge calibration. A critical methodological concern: LLM-as-judge for caricature often inherits the caricatures it is supposed to detect. 2025 ACL workshops are addressing judge-side bias.
  • Policy and deployment. Caricature amplification is now cited in EU AI Act risk assessments for social science and polling tools that use LLM personas.

Harnessing Approaches

  • Prompt-time calibration. Counterfactual prompt sets (Cheng et al., EMNLP 2023) that sample the same persona across diverse contexts and re-weight final output by diversity score — simple but effective, 38% caricature reduction reported.
  • Post-hoc diversity reranking. Generate N candidate persona responses, re-rank by a dedicated "individuation classifier" trained on ground-truth demographic data (NeurIPS 2024).
  • Persona-aligned RLHF. Replace generic "helpful" reward with persona-specific reward models that jointly penalize exaggeration while rewarding fluency (Findings of ACL 2024).
  • Data curation. Filter persona fine-tuning data against empirical survey distributions to break the amplification loop (Wang et al., 2024).
  • Individual-level grounding. Replace group labels with real response histories where available; Twin-2K-500 shows that individual digital twins can predict behavior at both individual and aggregate levels (Toubia et al., 2025).
  • Validation gate before simulation. Validate synthetic responses against a human benchmark and restrict claims to the tested design space, as in the bank-run study (Kazinnik, 2026); unvalidated personas should not be read as population estimates.
  • Human–AI hybrid workflows. Use LLM-generated respondents to expand coverage, but keep humans responsible for sampling, interpretation, and final decisions — hybrids outperform either alone (Arora et al., 2025).
